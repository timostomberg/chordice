Chordice Help

What Chordice does

Chordice generates chord progressions for practice. Press New Song for new rows with the same settings. Changing one option changes only that option.

The toolbar

  • Options button: shows the options panel, or hides it when it is shown.
  • Exercises button: shows the exercises in place of the options, or hides them when they are shown.
  • Help button: shows this help in place of the options, or hides it when it is shown. Like the instrument tabs, only one of the Options, Exercises and Help buttons is pressed at a time. A click on the toolbar outside its buttons hides or shows the panel. On a touch screen, a swipe to the right from the left edge shows the panel, a swipe to the left on it hides it, and the back button hides it too.
  • Instrument tabs: the piano (left, default) shows the notes of a clicked chord; the guitar (right) shows a guitar diagram. See The notes window.
  • Roman numerals button: shows or hides the roman numerals.
  • Chord tones button: shows the notes of each chord. Greyed out on the guitar. See Viewing the chords.
  • Colours button: shows or hides the background colours.
  • Dark mode button: switches the theme.

The circle of fifths

The circle of fifths left of the chords (above them on a narrow screen) chooses the key: click one major key. Its relative minor is shown inside, e.g. C and Am. It stays there while the side panel is closed.

The arrow button in the middle of the circle runs through all twelve keys: there are then 12 progressions, one per key, counterclockwise on the circle of fifths from C (C, F, B♭, E♭, …; F♯ or G♭ at random). Each row starts with its key, e.g. F (Dm) · Pop Progression. The circle is greyed out meanwhile and shows no key. All other settings stay as they are.

The options panel

Click a section's title to open or close it. A click on the chords area outside the chords hides the panel.

Progressions

Select the progressions by genre, with the group buttons or one by one. The number button sets how many progressions appear in a sequence: 1, 2, 4 or 8. Examples in C:

Progression variations

Rotations and Permutations (0 to 5): how likely a progression is to start at another chord, and how likely the order of the progressions is to be shuffled. 0 means never, 5 means always.

Chord variations

Chips choose the chord extensions, such as 7, maj7 or add9. Amount (0 to 5) is how likely a chord is to take one. The #b switch, when off, leaves out extensions with tones foreign to the key; in C the 7 is left out because B♭ is foreign, but add9 stays.

Bass variations

Amount (0 to 5): how likely a chord is to get a bass note other than its root, e.g. C/E. Greyed out on the guitar.

Melody variations

The Melody button switches the inversions on. Then each chord is shown in one of its inversions, e.g. C/E, chosen by its top note, which moves smoothly from chord to chord. Strength (0 to 5): how far the top notes may jump. 0 keeps them as close as possible, 5 makes every inversion equally likely. Greyed out on the guitar.

Exercises

The Exercises button in the toolbar shows the exercises in place of the options. An exercise fixes the options for one kind of practice; click it again to switch it off, and your own settings come back. While it is on, the options it fixes are greyed out, and their sections cannot be opened; the arrow button in the circle of fifths is fixed too. Circle around ii7-V7-Imaj7 plays the Jazz Cadence once in every key, round the circle of fifths, with only the 7 and maj7 variations at Amount 5, and no rotations, permutations or bass variations. The melody variations stay free.

New Song

The New Song button (bottom right) creates new rows with the current settings.

Viewing the chords

  • Colours: the background shows the harmonic function: tonic, subdominant or dominant.
  • Blue tones: notes a chord extension adds. Red tones: notes foreign to the key.
  • Square brackets: the top note, shown as the melody note when the Melody button is on.

The notes window

Click a chord (or press Enter or Space on it) to open its notes. Click the left half of the window for the previous inversion or shape, the right half for the next one, or swipe: to the left for the next one, to the right for the previous one. The arrow keys do the same. Escape closes the window.

  • Piano: the notes on a staff, with the bass as slash bass or root.
  • Guitar: a diagram of the chord in standard tuning. If the chord has no exact shape, the most similar chord is shown.

Saved settings

Your settings are saved in a cookie in this browser for one year, and in a copy in its local storage, so the page opens as you left it. The progression selection is the exception: all progressions are selected each time the page opens. Safari on iPhones and iPads forgets them after seven days without a visit; added to the home screen, Chordice keeps them.

On a phone or tablet

  • Home screen: add Chordice to the home screen (in Safari: Share, then Add to Home Screen) and it opens as an app of its own, without the bars of the browser.
  • Without a network: once opened online, Chordice also works offline, e.g. in a rehearsal room.
  • Screen: while Chordice is shown, the screen stays on, so it does not go dark while you play.
  • Zoom: pinch the chords to make them larger or smaller.
