Chordice Help

What Chordice does

Chordice generates chord progressions for practice. Press Generate for new rows with the same settings. Changing one option changes only that option.

The toolbar

  • Options button: shows or hides the options panel.
  • Instrument tabs: the piano (left, default) shows the notes of a clicked chord; the guitar (right) shows a guitar diagram. See The notes window.
  • Roman numerals button: shows or hides the roman numerals.
  • Chord tones button: shows the notes of each chord. See Viewing the chords.
  • Colours button: shows or hides the background colours.
  • Dark mode button: switches the theme.

The options panel

Click a section's title to open it. Only one section is open at a time. A click on the chords area opens Keys.

Keys

Choose one major key on the circle of fifths. Its relative minor is shown in brackets, e.g. C (Am).

The arrow button beside Keys runs through all twelve keys: there are then 12 progressions, one per key, counterclockwise on the circle of fifths from C (C, F, B♭, E♭, …; F♯ or G♭ at random). Each row starts with its key, e.g. F (Dm) · Pop Progression. Keys is greyed out and cannot be opened meanwhile. All other settings stay as they are.

Progressions

Select the progressions by genre, with the group buttons or one by one. The number button sets how many progressions appear in a sequence: 1, 2, 4 or 8. Examples in C:

Progression variations

Rotations and Permutations (0 to 5): how likely a progression is to start at another chord, and how likely the order of the progressions is to be shuffled. 0 means never, 5 means always.

Chord variations

Chips choose the chord extensions, such as 7, maj7 or add9. Amount (0 to 5) is how likely a chord is to take one. The #b switch, when off, leaves out extensions with tones foreign to the key; in C the 7 is left out because B♭ is foreign, but add9 stays.

Bass variations

Amount (0 to 5): how likely a chord is to get a bass note other than its root, e.g. C/E. Greyed out on the guitar.

Melody variations

The Melody button switches the inversions on. Then each chord is shown in one of its inversions, e.g. C/E, chosen by its top note, which moves smoothly from chord to chord. Amount (0 to 5): how far the top notes may jump. 0 keeps them as close as possible, 5 makes every inversion equally likely. Greyed out on the guitar.

Exercises

An exercise fixes the options for one kind of practice; click it again to switch it off, and your own settings come back. While it is on, the options it fixes are greyed out, and their sections cannot be opened. Circle around ii7-V7-Imaj7 plays the Jazz Cadence once in every key, round the circle of fifths, with only the 7 and maj7 variations at Amount 5, and no rotations, permutations or bass variations. The melody variations stay free.

Generate

The Generate button (bottom right) creates new rows with the current settings.

Viewing the chords

  • Colours: the background shows the harmonic function: tonic, subdominant or dominant.
  • Blue tones: notes a chord extension adds. Red tones: notes foreign to the key.
  • Square brackets: the top note, shown as the melody note when the Melody button is on.

The notes window

Click a chord (or press Enter or Space on it) to open its notes. Click the left half of the window for the previous inversion or shape, the right half for the next one. The arrow keys do the same. Escape closes the window.

  • Piano: the notes on a staff, with the bass as slash bass or root.
  • Guitar: a diagram of the chord in standard tuning. If the chord has no exact shape, the most similar chord is shown.

Saved settings

Your settings are saved in a cookie in this browser for one year, so the page opens as you left it. The progression selection is the exception: all progressions are selected each time the page opens.
